### 團體自學 — THE CATEGORY ERROR, found 2026-10-21. READ THIS BEFORE ANY SECTION.

Taiwan's 非學校型態實驗教育 has **THREE** forms, not one: **個人** (individual),
**團體** (group) and **機構** (institution). The 團體 form is an **自學互助會**, a
mutual-aid self-learning association founded jointly by the parents of **THREE OR
MORE STUDENTS** and capped at **THIRTY people** in total.
**WHY THIS MATTERS MORE THAN ALMOST ANYTHING ELSE IN THIS FILE:** for five weeks
the digest treated this family as individual self-learners facing institutions
that register classes rather than students, while separately recording
"teammates" as the top obstacle. 團體自學 addresses both at once — a group is a
recognised 實驗教育 unit, plausibly with a 指導老師 and a roster, which is what
Bebras, Conrad, the Ethics Olympiad and the World Scholar's Cup each ask for in
different words. **STOP ASKING ORGANISERS "can an individual self-learner
register?" and START ASKING "what do you accept from a 團體自學 group?"**
NOT VERIFIED, and must not be asserted: how any organiser actually treats a 團體;
whether forming one is practical or worth the administrative cost for this
family; the county application calendar for 團體自學.

### Section 1 findings logged 2026-10-16

**PHYSOLY IS COMMUNITY-BUILT — disclose it the way Omega Learn is disclosed.**
https://physoly.tech/ and https://physoly.tech/kalda/ — a free hub of notes,

### Section 4 — A PUBLISHER-HOSTED EXCERPT IS MARKETING, NOT RECEPTION (2026-11-19)

For _Sniff_ the favourable material comprised: a **cover blurb** (Catherine Clarke), and
an excerpt of a *Literary Review* notice **hosted on the publisher's own publicity
page**. Neither is reception. The only real review reached was the *Spectator
Australia*'s, which was mildly critical on structure.

**THE RULE: a quotation from a review, republished by the publisher, counts as
marketing until the review itself is read.** The selection is the publisher's, so it
carries no information about the review's overall verdict — a demolition with one warm
sentence in it looks identical from the publicity page. **Cite such a line only for its
CONTENT (an idea worth passing on), never as evidence of how the book was received**,
and say which it is. The _Literary Review_'s "olfactory world of simulacra" line was used
on exactly that basis today.

**And state the honest shape when it is thin:** "one real review, mildly critical on
structure; everything else is a blurb" is a complete and useful answer. The prompt
forbids dressing blurbs as reception; it does not require pretending reception exists.

### Section 4 — THE STRUCTURAL CRITICISM, a dissent type worth seeking out (2026-11-15)

Kirkus on Firestein: "a solid and even stirring defense of science" — **and then that the
optimism RECEDES IN THE SECOND HALF as the author presses his case against determinism.**
The book's advertised subject gives way to its actual argument.

**THAT IS THE MOST USEFUL SHAPE OF DISSENT TO LOOK FOR, and it should be preferred over
disagreement when both are available.** It is not "I think the author is wrong"; it is
**"the book you wrote is not the book your title promised"** — a claim about the object
rather than about the thesis, checkable by a reader, and exactly the move a student
writing about arguments needs to learn. Two more instances already in the file: the
"Shame Code" objection to Appiah (the title names the wrong force, 6 Nov) and the
"wide-ranging" note on Firestein (praise that hints the argument accumulates rather than
builds).

**SO WHEN SEARCHING FOR DISSENT, SEARCH FOR THE STRUCTURAL OBJECTION SPECIFICALLY** —
reviews that admire a book and still say its parts do not do what its frame claims. It
is more common than outright opposition and more instructive than either.

### Section 4 — WHAT TO DO WHEN NO CRITIQUE EXISTS, established 2026-11-13

For Al-Khalili's _On Time_ **every reachable review was positive** — Nature's brief

**THE ANSWER THAT WORKED, and it generalises: present the LIVE DISAGREEMENT THE BOOK
IS A MOVE IN, not a manufactured review section.** _On Time_ argues the arrow of time
is real and is written partly against the "time is not fundamental / does not exist"
camp. Naming that opposing position, and saying a reader should read one of its books
alongside, gives the family the actual other side — which is what the dissent

**AND MARK IT AS YOUR OWN FRAMING.** That paragraph was explicitly labelled as mine
rather than as sourced criticism, because the opposing authors' current statements were
not checked that day. **This is the same distinction that failed on IASC on 12 Nov** —
a remembered framing presented with the authority of a read source. Drawing the line

STRONG LIST rather than dropping the item.** One of the three "key ideas" (that the

### Section 4 — THE MOVING ATTRIBUTION. A sourcing lesson, 2026-11-25.

The Werth entry's best demonstration. **The publisher's page carries an admiring quotation
about the book being "a warning about conflicts playing out again today". In one search it was
attributed to SCIENCE; in another, to the NEW YORK TIMES.** Same page, two readings, two
outlets.

**SO THE QUOTE WAS PRINTED WITH NO ATTRIBUTION AT ALL, and the reason was stated in the email.**
This is the strongest available argument for the 19 Nov rule that **a publisher-hosted review
excerpt is MARKETING, NOT RECEPTION** — not because publishers lie, but because the excerpt is
detached from its source and an excerpt's provenance degrades the moment it is lifted.
**THE TEST, now standing: if a praise quotation's OUTLET cannot be pinned down on two tries,
print the praise without the outlet or not at all — never pick one.** (Standing Rule 5 says
report conflicts and never pick; a conflict about WHO SAID IT is still a conflict.)

**AND THE COUNTERWEIGHT, so the rule does not become a reason to drop all reception:**
a TRADE VERDICT is reception. *Publishers Weekly*'s starred review is a judgement by a named
trade publication with a known process, and it belongs in the entry even when the publisher is
the one quoting it, because the STARRED designation is a fact about PW rather than a sentence
lifted out of a review.

### Section 4 — THE CROSS-LIST TRAP, recorded 2026-11-04

A rank looked up for one list can be the rank from a **different list** by the same
kind of publication. Confirmed cases: _Wolf Hall_ is **#3** on the NYT 100 Best Books
of the 21st Century, **1st** on the Guardian's own 2019 list, and **6th** on
Vulture's. _Austerlitz_ is **#8** on the NYT list and **5th** on the Guardian's.

**So a rank is only a rank once the LIST is named in the same source.** A bare
"ranked third" in a search summary is worthless. When a number cannot be tied to a
named list, say the rank is unverified rather than printing it.

## Section 5 — Competitions for the 11-year-old

The younger child is **11** and lives in **Taiwan**. Both facts are filters and
they bite harder here than anywhere else in this digest, because most published
"competitions for kids" lists are US-school-centric.

### Four filters, all required

1. **Age/grade** — an 11-year-old must be eligible *now*. Say which division or
   level they enter (Math Kangaroo Level 3 / Benjamin, Bebras Benjamin, Bow Seat
   Junior). A contest they age into in two years is a "mark the date", labelled
   as such.
2. **Reachable from Taiwan** — either run in Taiwan, run online with
   international entry, or postal/submission-based. If entry runs through a
   national organiser, **name the step** (find the Taiwan coordinator) rather
   than implying the family can just sign up.
3. **Entry route for a homeschooler** — the single most common blocker. Many
   contests register *sites and schools*, not students. State plainly whether a
   **parent can register the child directly**, whether a registered school or
   test centre is required, or whether a team and coach are needed.
4. **Cost and deadline** — exact, and never invented. Free rounds are the best
   finds and should be led with.

### Section 6 — OPEN-SOURCE CONTRIBUTION, examined properly 2026-11-18

**IT PRODUCES THE MOST DURABLE ARTEFACT IN THE WHOLE POOL.** A merged pull request is a
permanent, dated, publicly attributable change to something other people use, with the
contributor's name on it, the reviewer's beside it, **and the argument about whether to
accept it preserved in public underneath.** Not a certificate, not a database row, not a
communiqué. On the credit tier below it belongs at or near the top with eBird.

**THE AGE RULE — A LAW, NOT A GATEKEEPER.** GitHub's own Terms of Service and GitHub
Education's terms both set **13 for a personal account**; GitHub says it does not target
under-13s and terminates accounts it learns of. A 2017 policy thread ties this to COPPA
(dated secondary source — the reason is probable, the rule firm).

**THE CAVEAT THAT WAS NOT EXPECTED: GITHUB'S OWN TERMS SAY "YOUR COUNTRY'S MINIMUM AGE
MAY BE OLDER."** So 13 is a floor and Taiwan's own figure is **UNESTABLISHED**. It does
not bite today — the elder child is past any plausible floor, the 11-year-old is under
13 either way — **but check Taiwan's position when the 11-year-old turns 13 rather than
assuming the American number.** Generalise it: *an international service's stated
minimum age may be the US minimum, not the local one.*

**WHAT A FIRST CONTRIBUTION IS:** documentation fixes, a clearer error message, a missing
test, a README typo. **The skill is not programming — it is reading someone else's
codebase and contributing guidelines well enough to make a change they will accept**,
which is the 中文維基百科 discipline rather than the CS50x one.

**LIMIT CLOSED 2026-11-21/22, WITH NUMBERS MEASURED OFF GITHUB'S OWN SEARCH API** (`gh api
search/issues`, so primary data rather than an article about the practice):
- `label:"good first issue" state:open type:issue` → **476,769**
- `label:"help wanted"` → **556,090**
- `label:"good-first-issue"` (hyphenated) → **9,064** · `label:"beginner-friendly"` → **3,274**
- `label:"good first issue" state:open no:assignee` → **352,870**
- same, `created:>=2026-10-22` → **31,748** in the last 30 days

**TWO CONCLUSIONS, BOTH REUSABLE.** (1) **SEARCH THE SPACED THREE-WORD LABEL.** "good first
issue" is GitHub's own DEFAULT label text; the hyphenated variant finds about **2%** of the
pool, so a child searching the wrong spelling would conclude the opportunity barely exists.
**A naming convention can hide 98% of an opportunity** — the same shape as Standing Rule 7's
language problem, in a different register. (2) **THE LABEL IS A LIVE QUEUE, NOT A GRAVEYARD:**
~352k unassigned and ~32k fresh per month.

**THE LIMIT THAT REMAINS, AND IT IS THE REAL ONE: RESPONSIVENESS.** A label means a maintainer
TAGGED an issue, not that they will review a stranger's PR promptly or kindly — and that is
what decides whether a first contribution becomes an artefact or a dead branch. **A COUNT
CANNOT MEASURE IT, AND NO AMOUNT OF SEARCHING WILL.** Which is why this produced a new, and
now the EASIEST, rung on the section-3 department ladder: **"do you contribute to an
open-source project, and would you point at one issue a beginner could try."** A pointer from
someone who knows which maintainers answer is cheaper to give than any favour on the old
ladder and supplies the one thing research cannot.

**METHOD NOTE: `gh api search/issues` is available in this container and is PRIMARY DATA about
GitHub.** Secondary rate limits bite after a handful of queries in a minute — space them, and
expect a 403 telling you so. Prefer it to articles about open-source onboarding.

**HONEST PROPORTION:** one merged doc fix is real and is not an achievement. **A dozen
small accepted changes to one project over three months** shows persistence and the
ability to take review — same shape as 中文維基百科, where the threshold is trivial and
the thing worth having lies well beyond it. **And the timing note: this is an artefact a
recommender can describe in one sentence, which is a reason NOT to pretend starting it
three weeks before a deadline would help.**

### Section 6 — iNaturalist: THE LICENCE GATES GBIF ENTIRELY, established 2026-11-06

From iNaturalist's own guidance and forums:

- An observation becomes **research grade** when the community agrees on the ID.
- Research-grade observations are bulk-exported to **GBIF** roughly **weekly**.
- **Only if the licence permits.** CC0, CC BY and CC BY-NC are exported. **All rights
  reserved is excluded.** And — counter-intuitively — **CC BY-SA and CC BY-ND are
  ALSO excluded, because GBIF cannot process them.**
- Under CC BY, reusers must **credit the observer**.
- GBIF's "Recorded by" filter on the iNaturalist dataset uses the observer's
  **profile name**, not a user ID.

**THE ACTIONABLE FORM: this is a one-minute settings check, not a decision about
whether to participate.** A child can reach research grade on hundreds of
observations across a year and contribute nothing to any database if the licence is
left on all rights reserved — or on a Creative Commons licence that *sounds* more
generous and is in fact excluded. **CC BY both shares the data and compels
attribution.** Nobody tells you this.

**LIMITS, open:** no minimum age or account-age rule found for iNaturalist, exactly
as for eBird. The claim that citing a GBIF DOI discharges the attribution obligation
is a **forum post, not policy** — do not rely on it. Being in a database is not being
cited: most observations never are.

### STANDING INSIGHT, 2026-10-31 — A RATION IS NOT A LIMIT

The 2026 Hsinchu restriction gave its REASON: capacity. That distinction is now a
tool to apply everywhere in this file.

a rule about what you ARE. Asking changes nothing.

about SUPPLY. Rations have edges, waiting lists, and exceptions when a place opens.

### Section 8 — 22 NOV, AND A PAIR THAT WORKS

**22 Nov: "When an investigation looks into something and finds nothing, what has it found?"**
The move: separating *this did not happen* from *this was not established* — reported in the
same words (cleared, no evidence found, no action taken) and different claims. **The trap is
double and pulls both ways:** "nothing found" as proof nothing happened, OR as proof of a
cover-up. Both skip the useful question — **what could this inquiry have detected?** Who could
it compel to speak, what records could it read, what kind of event leaves no trace in either.
Run it on a domestic case (something missing, everyone asked, nobody admits) and write down what
the asking-around *could* and *could not* have discovered before arguing about who did it.

**THE PAIR, AND IT IS THE BEST CONTINUITY THE SECTION HAS PRODUCED:** 21 Nov asked how you know,
from inside a SINGLE CASE, what your evidence cannot show you; 22 Nov points the same question at
a PROCEDURE. **A formal inquiry is still a method, and a method's blind spots do not announce
themselves in its conclusion.** Six days now on one shape (17–22 Nov).
