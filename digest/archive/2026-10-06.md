## 1. Interactive learning platforms

**Physoly** — **free**. A hub of notes, handouts and problems from olympiads
worldwide, for high schoolers doing contest physics. Its most valuable feature:
**solution manuals to the Kalda handouts.** Jaan Kalda's handouts are the
canonical olympiad physics problem sets and are free; what they lack is worked
solutions, which is exactly what stops a self-studying student cold. Physoly fills
that gap and runs a Discord. <https://physoly.tech/> · Kalda's originals:
<https://www.ioc.ee/~kalda/ipho/>
*Limit:* no schedule, no deadline, no one checking. Highest-quality raw material
with zero structure — precisely what $895 buys and this does not.

**Omega Learn** — **free**. Its USAPhO guide covers format, eligibility, book
recommendations, free handouts and practice exams in one place. The map before
Physoly's territory. <https://www.omegalearn.org/usapho>
*Limit:* a guide, not a course.

> **Honest framing for Wednesday.** F=ma is 25 multiple-choice questions in 75
> minutes on algebra-based mechanics, taken by ~6,000 students, and it gates
> USAPhO. Kalda + Physoly + Omega Learn is genuinely *enough material* to prepare.
> What PhysicsWOOT adds is sixteen scheduled sessions, graded problems, and a
> former IPhO coach. **If the student already works through hard problem sets
> unsupervised, the free stack suffices and the $895 should stay in the bank. If
> not, the $895 buys the thing actually missing** — and Wednesday is the last day.

**Anki** — **free** on Windows, Mac, Linux, Android (AnkiDroid) and browser
(AnkiWeb), with free sync across all. iOS (AnkiMobile) is a **one-time $24.99
purchase**; no subscriptions, no storage fees, add-on ecosystem free. Includes
FSRS since 2023.
*Grade fit:* both children — Chinese characters and vocabulary for the younger;
formulas and constants for the elder.
*Limit:* rewards consistency, punishes gaps — two weeks off and the backlog
demoralises. It only stores what is already understood. **A retention tool, not a
learning tool**, and treating it as the latter is how people waste months.

## 4. Book of the day

### *À la recherche du temps perdu* (In Search of Lost Time) — Marcel Proust, 1913–1927

Ranked **#2** on Le Monde's list, behind Camus.

This closes an accidental three-book arc. On 2 October a neuroscientist argued
memory is a physical object that can be edited; on 3 October a novelist built a
book out of the same problem. **Proust is who both are ultimately arguing with,
and he got there in 1913.**

**The central argument.** The self is not a thing that persists through time; it
is something time keeps dissolving. The person you were is genuinely gone,
unreachable by deliberate recall — and yet it can return, whole and
involuntarily, triggered by a sensation. These returns are not nostalgia but *the
only access we have to our own continuity*, and the sole way to hold them is to
make art of them. The 3,000 pages are the story of a man becoming the person
capable of writing the book you are reading.

**Key ideas:**

1. **Involuntary memory.** The madeleine does what years of trying could not:
   returns the past intact, with its feeling attached. Deliberate recall gives
   *facts about* the past; involuntary memory gives the past. Proust's distinction
   is what engram research is, a century later, trying to find the biology of.
2. **Habit is an anaesthetic.** We stop seeing what we see daily. Most of life is
   lost not to forgetting but to never having been perceived — a claim about
   *attention*, far more practical than it sounds.
3. **Love is mostly projection, jealousy its engine.** Proust's lovers love an
   image they constructed; the beloved's inner life is unknowable, which is
   precisely what generates obsession. Unsentimental to the point of cruelty.
4. **Society is a theatre of self-deception.** Hundreds of pages of salons and
   snobbery — funny, merciless, and serious: people construct selves for audiences
   and then believe them.
5. **Art redeems time** — as technique, not consolation. The work of art is the
   only container preserving a moment with its texture intact, which is why the
   narrator's long apparent failure to become a writer *is* the plot.

> **Genuinely difficult.** Seven volumes, ~3,000 pages, page-long sentences, very
> little happening. Nobody should be told to "read Proust" as a project.
> **What works:** read *"Combray"* — the first hundred-odd pages of *Swann's Way*
> — then stop, guilt-free. It holds the madeleine, the goodnight kiss, and the
> whole method in miniature.
> **Translations:** Scott Moncrieff (rev. Kilmartin and Enright) is the classic
> English; the newer Penguin uses a translator per volume, Lydia Davis on *Swann's
> Way*, more exact and less lush. Good Chinese translations exist and are worth
> preferring if they read more naturally.

**Suitability:** for the elder child only if they genuinely like difficult prose —
not a book to assign. **Not for the 11-year-old.**
