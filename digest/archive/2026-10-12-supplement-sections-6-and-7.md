## Section 6 — International collaboration (even-numbered dates)

Added 2026-10-12 at the family's request ("add more international collaboration
opportunities"). **Appears on EVEN dates only.** 2–3 items.

**The distinction that defines the section:** Section 2 is about *going* somewhere.
Section 6 is about *working with people elsewhere without going anywhere* — which,
given how often residence filters close doors on this family, is the lowest-friction
category available to them.

### Filters
Same as section 2 on residence and citizenship, plus: collaboration must be real
(a shared artefact, dataset, manuscript, team or codebase — not a webinar
audience); entry route must be stated for a homeschooler; cost exact; and say
plainly when a thing confers no credential.

### Verified pool
- **Journal of Emerging Investigators** — grades 6–12 at submission, so BOTH
  children. Free to publish, no author fees, institutionally funded. Non-profit run
  by grad students, postdocs and professors across the US; founding tied to Harvard
  grad students, 2011. ~4–6 review stages with feedback from several professional
  scientists; 8–12 week reviews. 250+ papers since 2012; 70–75% of submissions
  reached publication over the past five years. **A SENIOR MENTOR MUST BE LAST
  AUTHOR** — this converts the standing lab-shadowing ask into a concrete request
  ("would you mentor a manuscript"). **CATCH:** one guide reports purely CS,
  data-mining and public-dataset-only projects are no longer accepted, which cuts
  against the computational-research route this digest has pushed. Scope described
  inconsistently (bio+physical only, versus bio+physical+engineering+social).
  ISEF ethics guidelines apply. <https://emerginginvestigators.org/>
- **Zooniverse** — free, global, real research data classified for actual research
  teams. 1.6M–2M+ volunteers depending on source/date. **No platform-wide age
  minimum found**; a 13+ figure came from one event listing, not a rule; NSTA says
  only a valid email is needed and most projects suit elementary/middle schoolers.
  Projects set their own rules. **Volunteer credit in papers could not be
  established — do not present it as a publication credential.**
  <https://www.zooniverse.org/>
- **FIRST Global Challenge** — ages 14–18, **one national team per country**,
  selected by an in-country partner; teams of 3–5 plus adult technical mentor and
  team organiser. Not an application but a national selection; the step is
  identifying **Taiwan's in-country partner**. FIRST's FAQ page is ~18 months old,
  so treat figures as probably-current. <https://first.global/faq/>

### Part B — a recurring problem to attempt
- **Advent of Code** — daily programming puzzles each December morning, free,
  global. **Caveats:** Wikipedia now gives the window as 1–12 December where older
  sources say 1–25, and the 2026 schedule is unconfirmed — plan for twelve days.
  **From 2025 onward only PRIVATE leaderboards are supported**; public global
  leaderboards retired, so the compete-with-the-world framing is gone. The value is
  dated puzzles of escalating difficulty, each a small artefact for a public repo.
  <https://adventofcode.com/>
- **Always-available version:** Project Euler for mathematical programming; for the
  elder child the Kalda handouts with Physoly's solution manuals. No registration,
  no gate, no season.

### Alternation and its mitigation
The family chose alternating days over two daily sections or folding in. The risk —
a deadline landing on an off day — was flagged and chosen anyway, so the mitigation
is mandatory: **ANY DATED ITEM FROM SECTION 6 OR 7 ALSO APPEARS IN THE DAILY
DEADLINE LEAD AT THE TOP, on every day, regardless of whose turn it is.** An off day
must never hide a deadline. That is how the September physics-olympiad window was
lost and it will not be the mechanism twice.
