## 1. Learning platforms

### Semantic Scholar, arXiv and Connected Papers — the free research stack, and the barrier it removes

Aimed squarely at section 2. RSI is a **research** programme, and applicants are
asked to show research capacity. The standard obstacle for a homeschooled student
in Taiwan is that reading the actual literature normally requires a university
library login. **That obstacle has quietly disappeared.**

- **Semantic Scholar** — free AI-powered search across the scientific literature,
  with **no university login and no subscription**. Beyond keyword search it maps
  citation relationships and generates TLDR summaries, letting a student trace how
  ideas connect across a field and spot where the open questions are.
- **arXiv** — open access preprints, especially physics, maths and computer
  science. Free, no account.
- **Connected Papers** — builds a visual graph of papers related to one you
  specify, so you see a field's *shape* rather than a list. Free tier limited to
  **5 graphs per month**.

> **Why this is strategic, not just useful:** an RSI application that names a
> specific research question and cites real papers reads completely differently
> from one saying "I am passionate about science." Almost every applicant is
> passionate. **Very few have read the literature.** That gap used to be an access
> problem and is now only an effort problem — which means it is available on the
> same terms as to a student at a research university.

**Limits, and they matter:** arXiv preprints are **not peer-reviewed** — anyone
can post, and some of it is wrong. TLDR summaries are machine-generated: an
index, not a substitute for reading. And a first paper is genuinely hard; the
usual advice is abstract → figures → conclusion → then decide whether to read the
middle.

<https://www.semanticscholar.org/> · <https://arxiv.org/> ·
<https://www.connectedpapers.com/>

## 4. Book of the day

### *Demon Copperhead* — Barbara Kingsolver (2022)

On the NYT's 100 Best Books of the 21st Century. Winner of the 2023 Pulitzer
Prize for Fiction.

> ⚠️ **Content note:** addiction, child neglect, death. Heavy — but reviewers
> consistently note it is also funny and full of love, "seething with love and
> outrage." Not punishing in the way Coetzee is.

**The central argument.** Kingsolver transposes Dickens's *David Copperfield* —
his study of institutional poverty in Victorian England — onto the opening years
of Appalachia's opioid epidemic, with an addicted Appalachian orphan as narrator.
**The structural claim is the transposition itself:** the machinery that grinds up
poor children is essentially the same across 170 years and an ocean. Only the
industries change. She dedicates the book to survivors of the opioid crisis and of
foster care.

**Key ideas:**

1. **Extraction is the through-line.** Logging, coal, big-box retail, and finally
   Purdue Pharma — each industry takes something out of Appalachia and leaves.
   Kingsolver's move is to put opioids in *that sequence* rather than treating
   them as a separate tragedy. Purdue extracted health.
2. **Addiction as something done *to* a region.** The book reverses the usual
   moral framing: not a community that chose poorly, but one that was targeted,
   because poverty and physical labour made it a promising market.
3. **Foster care as an institution that processes children rather than raising
   them.** Demon passes through it the way Dickens's orphans passed through
   workhouses.
4. **Voice is the argument.** Demon narrates in his own idiom throughout. The
   insistence that Appalachians speak for themselves — rather than being
   explained by outsiders — is the book's quiet structural claim about "a
   community too often stereotyped or ignored."
5. **Dickens still works because the problem still exists.** If a Victorian plot
   maps this cleanly onto 21st-century America, the uncomfortable implication is
   that very little was actually solved.

> **Pairs directly with *Bad Influence* (2026-09-20).** Cohen described the
> **mechanism** — commerce colonising health until medicine and marketing are
> indistinguishable. Kingsolver shows the same mechanism **from the receiving
> end**, in one region, through one child. OxyContin marketed into Appalachia *is*
> medicine-as-marketing at industrial scale. The strongest pairing this rotation
> has produced: one supplies the structure, the other the cost.

**Accessibility:** long but propulsive, and the voice carries you. Considerably
easier reading than its subject matter suggests.
